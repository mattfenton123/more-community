01Executive Summary

More Community

More Community is a community infrastructure platform that helps people build, run and discover real-life communities. We exist to solve a problem that is both deeply personal and structurally significant: the people who run the grassroots communities that hold towns together are overwhelmed, under-supported and relying on a patchwork of disconnected tools.

We are building a single platform — free for community founders — that combines discovery, event management, membership, communication and analytics in one place. At the same time, we give people looking for connection a trusted directory of local groups, events and experiences.

02Founder Story

The greatest transformation of my life came through community.

For years I chased achievement through business, endurance sport and personal goals. I competed in Ironman triathlons, completed 12 Months 4 Charity challenges, and pushed myself through burnout again and again — mistaking relentless forward motion for progress. The most meaningful transformation didn't come from crossing a finish line. It came through recovery, honest conversations, men's groups, counselling skills training and the simple, powerful act of walking alongside others.

"Achievement changed my circumstances. Community changed my life."

I trained as a Mental Health First Aider, studied counselling skills and began facilitating men's groups and community walks. Through that process I discovered something that no achievement had ever given me: a deep sense of belonging. More Community was born from that realisation. Not as a tech product looking for a problem — but as a lived experience that revealed a structural gap. When I began building More Community in Tunbridge Wells, the tools problem became impossible to ignore.

03The Problem

Three interconnected problems. One platform.

Problem 1: The Founder's Burden

Building More Community exposed a bigger problem.

To run a single community, I needed WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, a website, forms, email, analytics, event tools, membership systems and challenge tracking. No single platform existed for community founders.

  • WhatsApp & Facebook for communication
  • Instagram for promotion
  • Eventbrite for events
  • Google Forms for RSVPs
  • Mailchimp for updates
  • Google Analytics for data
  • Custom website for credibility
Problem 2: The Invisible Leaders

Community leaders are the hidden infrastructure of society.

Every town has walking groups, running clubs, volunteering projects, faith groups, parent groups and entrepreneur communities. The people running them are overwhelmed, under-supported and relying on disconnected tools.

Problem 3: The Discovery Gap

Communities exist everywhere. Finding them is difficult.

People looking for connection rely on Facebook, Google, word of mouth or luck. Incredible groups already exist in every town — they are simply fragmented and hard to discover.

09Business Model

Free for leaders. Always.

Our core principle is non-negotiable: community founders never pay to use More Community. This is not a freemium strategy with a paywall behind it — it is a structural commitment that drives trust, adoption and network effects.

"We monetise the ecosystem, not the leader."

Revenue is generated through the value created around active communities — not by taxing the people who create that value. By combining hyper-local sponsorship, an experiences marketplace, premium SaaS tools and B2G enterprise contracts (NHS Social Prescribing), the model supports venture-scale economics targeting £14M+ ARR within 5 years — profitable from Year 2.

Experiences Marketplace

The Experiences Marketplace is a commission-based revenue stream that leverages the trust community leaders have with their members. Leaders browse a curated catalogue of local and national experiences — skydiving, spa retreats, theatre tickets, wine tastings, coasteering, pottery workshops — and promote them directly to their community. More Community earns a 10–15% base commission (industry standard for experience aggregators), while leaders set their own markup (typically 5–20%), creating a direct earning opportunity that incentivises promotion.

Year 1 focuses on single-day experiences, priced at an average of £50–£120 per person. From Year 2, multi-day packages (weekend retreats, adventure trips) are introduced, significantly increasing average transaction value. At scale, this stream generates approximately £3,000 per town per year, contributing £1.5M by Year 5 across 500 towns.

Why This Works

Community leaders are trusted curators. When a yoga leader recommends a wellness retreat or an adventure group promotes coasteering, members convert at significantly higher rates than anonymous marketplace listings. The leader’s endorsement is the marketing — More Community provides the infrastructure.

Market Size

Community infrastructure is an emerging category with significant structural tailwinds.

£4.2B TAM — Global Community Platform Market (2026)
£680M SAM — UK Local Community & Social Prescribing
£14.3M SOM — 500 UK Towns × Revenue Target

TAM — £4.2B

The global community platform market is projected at $5.3B (£4.2B) in 2026, growing at 13.7% CAGR (Grand View Research). This includes enterprise community platforms, event management and social prescribing infrastructure across developed markets.

SAM — £680M

Our serviceable market is the UK local community infrastructure segment: ~1,800 towns with 10,000+ population, 42 NHS ICSs requiring social prescribing platforms, ~5.5M small businesses spending on local marketing, and the emerging council data insights market.

SOM — £14.3M

Our initial achievable market is 500 towns within 5 years, each generating ~£28.6k blended revenue from sponsorship, ticketing, SaaS, data and experiences. At 500 towns this represents £14.3M revenue — roughly 30% of SOM, with significant room for pricing uplift and new revenue streams.

Why This Market Is Defensible

Community infrastructure has a powerful network effect: each active community in a town attracts more members, which attracts more communities, which attracts more sponsors. Once a town is "live" with 10+ communities, switching costs become very high — creating a natural geographic monopoly.

The Town Launch Playbook

To achieve our target of 500 towns, we have developed a repeatable, 12-week town launch playbook. In Years 1–3, launches are driven by our regional Community Managers (high-touch). In Years 4–5, we transition to a hybrid model where existing leaders drive organic expansion into adjacent towns via our "Community Accelerator" programme.

Phase 1: Seed (Weeks 1-2)
  • Identify 3-5 existing community leaders in the town
  • Personal outreach and platform onboarding by Community Manager
  • Map existing communities (Facebook Groups, council listings)
Phase 2: Activate (Weeks 3-6)
  • Leaders create community profiles, list first events
  • More Community runs 1 flagship event (e.g., community walk)
  • Local press and social media launch campaign
Phase 3: Monetise (Weeks 6-12)
  • Approach local businesses for tiered sponsorship
  • Connect with council wellbeing/social prescribing team
  • Target: 10+ active communities, 3+ signed sponsors
Phase 4: Self-Sustain (Month 4+)
  • Leaders run operations autonomously
  • Community Manager shifts to light-touch, regional support
  • Sponsor renewals handled by regional sales team
