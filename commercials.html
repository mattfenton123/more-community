Revenue Stream Analysis

Local Sponsorship (Core — 45% of Yr5 Revenue)

Each town generates ~£13,000/year from 3-4 local business sponsors across tiered packages (Headline £5-6k, Support £2-3k). This is priced well below the average UK SME annual marketing budget of £38,000 (Statista 2025). Sponsors receive branded presence across community events, the local microsite, and in-app placements. This stream scales linearly with town count and is the commercial backbone of the model.

NHS & Council Contracts (Enterprise — 10% of Yr5)

The NHS Long Term Plan mandates social prescribing across all 42 Integrated Care Systems (ICSs). More Community provides the digital infrastructure for these programmes — connecting link workers to community activities. At £50k per contract (below typical NHS SaaS procurement), this is a high-margin, sticky B2G revenue stream with multi-year renewal rates.

Council Performance Dashboard (Growth — 17% of Yr5)

From Year 3, we provide local authorities with a real-time community health and engagement dashboard. Councils currently pay £10k–£20k for static, manual community audit reports; More Community replaces this with live, anonymised data on local connection and wellbeing for £5k per town per year. This represents high-margin recurring revenue that grows with the network without incremental sales effort.

Experiences Marketplace (New — 10% of Yr5)

Community leaders curate and promote day experiences — skydiving, retreats, away days, theatre, wellness — to their groups. More Community earns a 10–15% base commission (industry standard for experience aggregators like Viator/GetYourGuide), while leaders choose their own markup (typically 5–20%), creating a direct earning opportunity that incentivises promotion. Year 1 focuses on single-day experiences; Year 2 expands to multi-day packages. At scale, this stream generates approximately £3,000 per town per year.

05Competitive Landscape

No platform does what we do.

The competitive landscape is fragmented. Existing platforms address individual elements of community — event management, social networking, communication — but none combine founder tools, local discovery and real-world community infrastructure in a single free platform. Below is an in-depth analysis of eight key competitors.

Direct
Nextdoor
Hyperlocal Social Network
The largest neighbourhood network globally. Verified-residency model creates trust but the platform is advertising-driven, creating a cluttered, Facebook-like experience. Limited community tools — no event ticketing, no leader analytics, no community creation workflow.
💰 Revenue: Advertising-led. Neighbourhood Ads, sponsored posts. Users are the product. Recently achieved profitability through AI-powered ad optimisation.
🎯 Our edge: Community-first not ad-first. Purpose-built founder tools. Free for leaders. No advertising clutter.
Direct
Meetup
Event Discovery Platform
Once the go-to for in-person groups, now suffering from aggressive monetisation since its 2024 Bending Spoons acquisition. Organisers pay subscriptions, members pay ~$2 per RSVP or $5/mo for access. Many organisers are actively seeking alternatives.
💰 Revenue: Double paywall. Organiser subscriptions ($22-35/mo) + member RSVP fees ($2/event) or Meetup+ ($5/mo). Event-first, not community-first.
🎯 Our edge: Always free for leaders. Community-first not event-first. Built for ongoing relationships, not one-off meetups.
Adjacent
Mighty Networks
Online Community Platform
White-label platform for creators building paid online communities. Strong for courses and digital content, but designed for virtual-first communities. No local discovery, no geo features, no real-world event infrastructure.
💰 Revenue: SaaS subscriptions. Community Plan $41/mo, Business $119/mo, Path-to-Pro $360/mo. Plus transaction fees on member payments.
🎯 Our edge: Built for in-person communities, not online courses. Free for leaders. Local-first discovery. Integrated event ticketing.
Adjacent
Circle
Community Platform for Creators
Modern community platform popular with SaaS companies and creators. Excellent discussion and content features, but fundamentally an online platform. No local community features, no geographic discovery, no event ticketing system.
💰 Revenue: SaaS tiers. Basic $89/mo, Professional $199/mo, Business $399/mo, Enterprise custom. No free tier for community leaders.
🎯 Our edge: Designed for real-world groups, not online forums. Free for community leaders. Location-based discovery that connects people locally.
Indirect
Discord
Communication Platform
Originally for gamers, now widely used for online communities. Powerful chat and voice features but overwhelming for non-technical users. No local discovery, no event management, no member analytics. Users are anonymous, not verified.
💰 Revenue: Nitro subscriptions ($9.99/mo), server boosts, and recently app marketplace. Free tier is generous but lacks community management tools.
🎯 Our edge: Simple UX for all ages. Real identity. Local discovery. Integrated event management and CRM. Purpose-built for real-world communities.
Adjacent
Hivebrite
Enterprise Community Management
White-label enterprise solution for alumni networks, associations and non-profits. Powerful features but prohibitively expensive for grassroots groups. Implementation timelines of 3-6 months. Not designed for hyperlocal communities.
💰 Revenue: Enterprise SaaS. Starting at ~$895/mo (Connect), with Scale and Enterprise tiers at custom pricing. Plus implementation/setup fees of £5-15k.
🎯 Our edge: Free and instant setup. Built for grassroots community leaders, not enterprise admins. Hyperlocal discovery and sponsorship tools included.
Indirect
Eventbrite
Event Management & Ticketing
The dominant event ticketing platform. Strong for one-off events but purely transactional — no community layer, no member relationships, no ongoing engagement tools. Each event is isolated with no continuity between them.
💰 Revenue: Transaction fees. 3.7% + £0.79 per paid ticket (UK). Free events are free to list. No community features, analytics or CRM at any tier.
🎯 Our edge: Community layer that creates ongoing member relationships. Free event ticketing with integrated CRM. Leaders keep attendees, not just ticket sales.
Direct
Facebook Groups
Social Network Feature
Still the default for many UK community groups, but rapidly declining among under-35s. Cluttered UX, algorithmic feed manipulation, no dedicated founder tools, no event ticketing, no analytics, and growing privacy concerns.
💰 Revenue: Indirect — Facebook Groups are a retention feature for Meta's advertising platform. Groups admin tools are minimal and underfunded.
🎯 Our edge: Purpose-built for founders with analytics, CRM, flyer gen. Clean UX. No ads. No algorithmic interference. Real local discovery.

Market Breakdown

TAM — £4.2B

The global community platform market is projected at $5.3B (£4.2B) in 2026, growing at 13.7% CAGR (Grand View Research). This includes enterprise community platforms, event management, and social prescribing infrastructure across developed markets.

SAM — £680M

Our serviceable market is the UK local community infrastructure segment: ~1,800 towns with 10,000+ population, 42 NHS ICSs requiring social prescribing platforms, ~5.5M small businesses spending on local marketing, and the emerging council data insights market.

SOM — £14.3M

Our initial achievable market is 500 towns within 5 years, each generating ~£28.6k revenue from sponsorship, ticketing, SaaS and data. At 500 towns this represents £14.3M revenue — roughly 30% of SOM, with significant room for pricing uplift and new revenue streams.

Why This Market Is Defensible
Community infrastructure has a powerful network effect: each active community in a town attracts more members, which attracts more communities, which attracts more sponsors. Once a town is "live" with 10+ communities, switching costs become very high. This creates a natural geographic monopoly — the same dynamic that made Nextdoor dominant in neighbourhood social networking.
08Pricing Strategy

Freemium done right.

Our pricing philosophy is simple: the people who create communities never pay. Revenue comes from the ecosystem around those communities. This maximises supply-side growth (more leaders → more communities → more members → more sponsor value).
